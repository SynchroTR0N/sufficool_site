# Diagnosis Phase: Understanding Your Results

The diagnosis phase can feel overwhelming. This guide helps you understand what's happening and what to expect next.

## Common Diagnostic Tests

### PSA Blood Test
- Measures prostate-specific antigen levels
- Elevated levels may indicate prostate problems
- Not specific to cancer alone

### Digital Rectal Exam (DRE)
- Physical examination of the prostate
- Can detect lumps or hard areas
- Quick and relatively comfortable procedure

### Prostate Biopsy
- Definitive test for cancer diagnosis
- Small tissue samples examined under microscope
- Provides Gleason score and staging information

## Understanding Your Results

When you receive your biopsy results, they will include:
- **Cancer confirmation**: Whether cancer cells were found
- **Gleason score**: Aggressiveness of the cancer
- **Location**: Where in the prostate cancer was found
- **Extent**: How much of the biopsy showed cancer

## Emotional Support

A cancer diagnosis is life-changing. It's normal to feel:
- Shocked or overwhelmed
- Anxious about the future
- Confused by medical terminology
- Urgent need to "do something"

Take time to process this information and don't hesitate to ask for support from family, friends, or counseling services.

## Next Steps

After diagnosis, you'll typically:
1. Meet with specialists (urologist, radiation oncologist)
2. Undergo additional staging tests if needed
3. Discuss treatment options
4. Take time to make decisions

Remember: Most prostate cancers are treatable, and you have time to make informed decisions about your care.
