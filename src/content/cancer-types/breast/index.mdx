# Breast Cancer Treatment Guide

Welcome to your comprehensive guide for understanding breast cancer treatment options. This resource provides evidence-based information to help you navigate your cancer journey with the latest clinical research and personalized treatment approaches.

## Understanding Breast Cancer

Breast cancer affects 1 in 8 women in their lifetime, with over 290,000 new cases diagnosed annually in the US. Modern treatment approaches have dramatically improved outcomes, with 5-year survival rates exceeding 90% for all stages combined.

### Breast Cancer Subtypes

Breast cancer treatment is increasingly personalized based on tumor biology:

#### Hormone Receptor-Positive (HR+) Breast Cancer
- **Prevalence**: 80% of breast cancers
- **Characteristics**: Express estrogen receptor (ER) and/or progesterone receptor (PR)
- **Treatment strategy**: Hormone-blocking therapy is cornerstone of treatment
- **Prognosis**: Generally excellent with appropriate endocrine therapy
- **Key consideration**: Risk of late recurrence (can occur greater than 10 years after diagnosis)

#### HER2-Positive Breast Cancer
- **Prevalence**: 15-20% of breast cancers
- **Characteristics**: Overexpression or amplification of HER2 protein
- **Treatment strategy**: Anti-HER2 targeted therapy dramatically improves outcomes
- **Prognosis**: Historically aggressive, now excellent outcomes with targeted therapy
- **Key drugs**: Trastuzumab, pertuzumab, T-DM1, T-DXd, neratinib

#### Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC)
- **Prevalence**: 15% of breast cancers
- **Characteristics**: Negative for ER, PR, and HER2
- **Treatment strategy**: Chemotherapy and immunotherapy (if PD-L1 positive)
- **Prognosis**: More aggressive but often responds well to chemotherapy
- **Special considerations**: Higher rates in BRCA1 carriers and younger women

### Molecular Profiling and Genomic Tests

#### Oncotype DX (21-gene assay)
- **Indication**: HR+, HER2-, node-negative or 1-3 positive nodes
- **Purpose**: Predict chemotherapy benefit
- **Recurrence Score interpretation**:
  - Low risk (0-25): Hormone therapy alone
  - Intermediate risk (26-100): Consider chemotherapy based on clinical factors
- **Evidence**: Reduces chemotherapy use by 30-40% without compromising outcomes

## Treatment by Stage

### Stage I Breast Cancer
**Treatment Approach**: Surgery followed by adjuvant therapy based on biology

#### Surgery:
- **Lumpectomy + sentinel lymph node biopsy**: Standard for most patients
- **Mastectomy**: Large tumor relative to breast size, multicentric disease, or patient preference
- **Oncoplastic surgery**: Combines cancer surgery with plastic surgery techniques

#### Adjuvant Therapy:
- **Radiation therapy**: Standard after lumpectomy (reduces recurrence by 70%)
- **Systemic therapy**: Based on tumor biology and genomic testing
  - HR+ disease: Hormone therapy ± chemotherapy (based on genomic testing)
  - HER2+ disease: Anti-HER2 therapy + chemotherapy
  - Triple-negative: Chemotherapy if tumor greater than 0.5cm

**Survival Outcomes**:
- Stage IA: 99% 5-year survival
- Stage IB: 98% 5-year survival

### Stage II Breast Cancer
**Treatment Approach**: Multimodal therapy with surgery and systemic treatment

#### Neoadjuvant vs. Adjuvant Therapy:
**Neoadjuvant chemotherapy** (before surgery) indicated for:
- Large tumors requiring mastectomy (may allow breast conservation)
- Node-positive disease
- Triple-negative breast cancer greater than 2cm
- HER2-positive disease
- Desire to assess treatment response

## Special Populations

### Young Women (less than 40 years)
**Unique Considerations**:
- **Genetic testing**: High likelihood of hereditary cancer syndromes
- **Fertility preservation**: Discuss before treatment initiation
- **Pregnancy considerations**: Special protocols for pregnancy-associated breast cancer
- **Higher risk biology**: Often triple-negative or HER2-positive
- **Psychosocial support**: Body image, dating, insurance concerns

### Hereditary Breast Cancer (BRCA1/2, other genes)
**Risk-reducing Strategies**:
- **Prophylactic mastectomy**: Reduces risk by greater than 90%
- **Prophylactic oophorectomy**: Reduces breast and ovarian cancer risk
- **Enhanced screening**: MRI + mammography starting age 25-30
